What is FIC?

A finance club, built by students who wished one existed sooner.

Our Mission

Why we exist, what we believe, and what makes a FIC chapter different from a regular club.

Mission

Teach investing the way we wish we'd been taught.

FIC exists to teach high school students how investing and finance actually work — through collaboration, hands-on lessons, and real-world application. Every chapter works toward the same finish line: a competitive stock pitch experience where students apply everything they've learned to a real company, in front of a real audience.

Vision

Financial literacy shouldn't be a privilege.

Right now, most people don't learn how to invest until college — if at all — and usually only if they happen to know someone who works in finance. We don't think that's fair. Every student deserves access to this knowledge, a low-stakes place to practice it, and a community that's figuring it out alongside them. Your zip code shouldn't decide whether you understand how money works.

What we stand for

The values that shape how every FIC chapter runs — from lesson plans to pitch day.

Curiosity

Good questions matter more than quick answers.

Integrity

Collaboration

Respect

Empathy

Curriculum Preview

Six lessons. Zero jargon.

Every FIC chapter works through the same six lessons, building toward the stock pitch competition.

1Intro to the Stock Market & Investing

What a stock actually is, how markets work, and why people invest in the first place — the foundation everything else builds on.

2Intro to Businesses & Why They Go Public

How companies are built, what it means to "go public," and why that matters to anyone holding a share of stock.

3Financial Statements

A plain-language walkthrough of income statements, balance sheets, and cash flow — the documents every investor needs to read.

4Valuing a Company

How investors decide what a company is actually worth, and how to start forming your own opinion on whether a stock is a good deal.

5Reading Charts & Evaluating Performance

How to read a stock chart, spot trends, and evaluate how a company has actually performed over time.

6Investor Psychology, Risk & Reward + Intro to Stock Pitching

Why investors make the decisions they do, how to think about risk versus reward, and how to put it all together into your first stock pitch.

Our Story

How FIC started, why it matters, and where it's headed next — in our founder's own words.

I started Future Investors Collective because of a question that kept bothering me: why don't we learn this in school? Not the vague "personal finance" stuff about balancing a checkbook — I mean actually understanding how the stock market works, how companies grow, and how to think about putting your own money somewhere.

By the time most people learn any of this, they're in college — and even then, only if they happen to take the right elective or stumble into the right club. For a lot of students, it never happens at all. It's not because the material is too hard. It's because almost nobody ever hands it to you in a way that makes sense.

So a group of us built that thing ourselves. We put together a curriculum that starts from zero — no assumptions, no jargon — and walks through everything from "what is a stock" to building a real investment pitch. We tested it, taught it, and watched people who'd never thought about investing start asking genuinely sharp questions about companies they used to just scroll past.

We believe financial literacy shouldn't be reserved for kids whose parents work in finance, or for students at schools with the budget for a fancy elective. Every student deserves a real shot at understanding how money works — and a community to figure it out with.

That's why we're turning FIC into a chapter model. This summer, we're working with students at other schools to set up their own FIC chapters, using the same curriculum, leader guides, and structure we built and tested ourselves. Next school year, those chapters launch as clubs — and by spring, we're bringing everyone together for our first inter-school stock pitch competition.

If you've ever wished someone had just sat you down and explained how investing actually works — that's exactly what we're trying to build. We'd love for you to be part of it.

— [Founder Name]
Founder, Future Investors Collective

FAQ

Questions people usually ask

Do I need finance experience to start a chapter?

No. The curriculum is built for total beginners — for leaders and members alike. If you can read a lesson out loud and ask good questions, you're qualified.

How much time does it take?

One weekly meeting. That's it. The curriculum is paced so a chapter can work through all six lessons over a semester without feeling rushed.

What do I get when I start a chapter?

The full six-lesson curriculum, leader guides for running meetings, and access to the FIC network of other chapters — plus an invitation to the inter-school pitch competition.

Does it cost anything?

No. FIC is a nonprofit and the curriculum is free for chapters. We're entirely community-supported.

What if my school already has a finance or investing club?

That's great — FIC's curriculum and pitch competition can plug right into an existing club. Reach out and we'll figure out what makes sense together.
